KIH: Keyboard In my Hand

Mechatronics · team of 4 · team lead · Dec 2025

A glove-type wearable Hangul keyboard for eyes-free Korean text entry. 16 tactile switches are embedded across the phalanxes of a pair of gloves (8 per hand) and pressed by the thumbs. Input is sequential rather than chorded; repeated taps on the same button add strokes, mirroring the graphical derivation principle (가획) of Hangul. Enumerates as a plug-and-play USB HID keyboard — no drivers or companion app required.

Motivation

Three gaps drove the design:

  • VR/AR text entry, where physical keyboards break down;
  • affordable tactile input for Korean visually-impaired users;
  • posture-related VDT syndrome (7.05 M reported cases in Korea, 2024).

Results

  • KSPC 1.27 — a 26% improvement over frequency-blind layouts.
  • 9–15 WPM in pilot use across three users.
  • Per-user timing calibration stored in EEPROM.
  • Total BOM cost: ₩14,184.

Stack

Arduino Leonardo (ATmega32u4), native USB HID. Firmware in Python (89.7%) and C++ (10.3%). Hardware: 16 tactile switches, gloves, wiring harness. MIT license.

Recognition

  • Grand Prize, SNU Mechatronics Competition (Dec 2025).
  • Exhibited at the 15th SNU College of Engineering Creative Design Festival (2026).

Role

Team of 4, team lead. Led the design and pitch; earned faculty praise for a compelling presentation.
